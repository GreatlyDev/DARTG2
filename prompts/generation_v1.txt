You are rewriting a student response into a target U.S. English dialect for a controlled research benchmark.

Your goal is to produce ONE controlled dialect rewrite that preserves the exact propositional meaning and original student writing while adding meaningful, linguistically valid target dialect features at the surface level.

INPUTS
Prompt:
{PROMPT}

Anchor Response:
{ANCHOR_RESPONSE}

Target Dialect Family:
{DIALECT_FAMILY}

Approved Feature Inventory:
{FEATURE_INVENTORY}

Disallowed Features / Notes:
{DISALLOWED_FEATURES_AND_NOTES}

INSTRUCTIONS
1. Start from the anchor response and make controlled, local edits that insert documented target-dialect features. The rewrite should show real surface variation, not a minimal edit, near-copy, or one appended marker.
2. Do not add, remove, exaggerate, or alter any facts, claims, reasoning, opinions, or tone.
3. Use only documented features from the approved feature inventory for the target dialect family.
4. Do not use stereotypes, caricatures, exaggerated slang, mocking spellings, or unsupported dialect features.
5. Keep the response sounding like natural, plausible student writing at the same writing level as the anchor.
6. Keep the length close to the original response. Do not significantly shorten or expand it.
7. Do not correct, normalize, smooth, or improve the student's spelling, grammar, punctuation, capitalization, wording, sentence boundaries, ideas, argument quality, or writing sophistication unless a documented dialect feature explicitly requires that exact local change.
8. Preserve original student errors and roughness that are unrelated to the target dialect feature. Low-score and low-band responses must stay low-score/low-band in writing quality.
9. Do not replace ordinary words with synonyms unless that exact replacement is an approved feature in the inventory.
10. Do not smooth punctuation, spacing, capitalization, sentence boundaries, quotation marks, or student placeholders such as @CAPS1, @NUM1, or @LOCATION1.
11. Do not use eye-dialect contractions such as stayin', doin', talkin', 'bout, or 'cause unless the approved inventory explicitly lists that exact form as allowed.
12. Do not explain your choices.
13. Output only the rewritten response text.
14. Use 2-5 approved target-dialect feature placements when they can be used naturally without changing meaning. Do not rely on a single transformation, single marker, single contraction, or one isolated intensifier.
15. If fewer than two approved target-dialect feature placements can be applied safely, produce the best safe meaning-preserving rewrite using every licensed feature that naturally fits. Do not force unsupported features or clean up the student's writing to reach the feature target.
16. Do not make the candidate a near-copy of the anchor with only one extra token, one extra discourse marker, or one isolated intensifier.
17. Distribute feature placements across the response when possible, but only where the anchor licenses them naturally.
18. If producing multiple candidates for the same anchor, make each candidate distinct from the others by using different approved feature choices or different natural placements.
19. Do not chase an arbitrary token-change percentage if doing so would require paraphrasing, grammar/spelling cleanup, unsupported slang, or meaning drift.
20. Prefer a mix of distinct approved feature entries when several fit naturally. Do not rely only on broad/common markers when a more context-specific approved feature can be used without changing meaning.
21. Use percentage change only as a guardrail: below about 5% surface change is usually too minimal, 10%-20% is a preferred working range for short anchors when safely possible, and above 25% risks paraphrase or meaning drift.
22. For short anchors, include at least 2 clear published dialect feature realizations when safely possible. For longer anchors, prefer 3-5 clear feature realizations when the anchor and inventory naturally license them.

QUALITY CHECK BEFORE WRITING
Before producing the final answer, make sure:
- the meaning matches the anchor response
- no new information has been introduced
- no original information has been removed
- at least 2 approved dialect feature placements are present when the inventory and anchor meaning naturally allow them
- 2-5 approved feature placements are used when the inventory and anchor meaning naturally allow them
- the variant is not below the minimum surface-change guardrail unless the anchor genuinely licenses only a tiny safe change
- the variant does not exceed the upper surface-change guardrail in a way that turns into paraphrase
- changes are distributed across the response where possible, not localized to one spot
- feature choices come from distinct approved inventory entries when the anchor licenses that naturally
- every changed word or phrase is licensed by an approved inventory feature
- the rewrite is not just the anchor plus a short phrase at the beginning or end
- the student's original non-dialect spelling, grammar, punctuation, capitalization, sentence boundaries, and wording have not been cleaned up
- no disallowed feature is used

If no approved feature can be applied without changing meaning or cleaning up student writing, output exactly:
FAIL
